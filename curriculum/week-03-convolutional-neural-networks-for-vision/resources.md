# Week 3 — Resources

Curated, free where possible. You do not need all of these — pick what fits how you learn.

- [Stanford CS231n — Convolutional Neural Networks](https://cs231n.github.io/convolutional-networks/) — the canonical, careful CNN notes: arithmetic, receptive fields, layer patterns.
- [PyTorch — Training a Classifier (CIFAR-10) tutorial](https://pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/blitz/cifar10_tutorial.html) — a full end-to-end classification pipeline to pattern-match against.
- *Dive into Deep Learning* (d2l.ai), CNN chapters — convolutions, pooling, and modern architectures (LeNet, AlexNet, VGG, NiN, GoogLeNet, ResNet) with runnable code.
- He et al. (2016), *Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition* — read the degradation-problem figures and the residual-block motivation directly from the source.
- Zeiler & Fergus (2014), *Visualizing and Understanding Convolutional Networks* — what feature maps and filters actually represent; the reference for Challenge 1.
- [A guide to convolution arithmetic for deep learning (Dumoulin & Visin, 2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.07285) — the definitive illustrated reference for output shapes, strides, padding, dilation, and transposed convolution.
- torchvision.transforms documentation — the augmentation toolbox (RandomCrop, RandomHorizontalFlip, ColorJitter, and the newer AutoAugment/RandAugment).
- Karpathy, *A Recipe for Training Neural Networks* (blog) — a practical checklist (overfit-one-batch, sanity checks, ablations) that operationalizes Lecture 3's honesty rules.

> All external links are references, not endorsements. Prefer primary sources (papers, official docs) when they exist.
